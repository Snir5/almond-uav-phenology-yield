  Key findings:

  Key findings:
  • Most trees show near-zero sensitivity per additional chill portion,

  • The R² map (Fig. 8D) identifies trees whose inter-annual EBI variation

  9. EBI TAIL (QUARTILE) RESPONSE TO CLIMATE (Fig. 9)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Q1: mean sensitivity = -0.00141  (n=308)
  Q2: mean sensitivity = -0.00105  (n=314)
  Q3: mean sensitivity = 0.00034  (n=312)
  Q4: mean sensitivity = 0.00120  (n=300)

  Key findings:
  • Q4 trees (persistently high EBI) show the largest sensitivity to chilling
    across most climate variables — their bloom brightness is amplified in
    cold winters and suppressed in warm winters, relative to low-EBI trees.
  • Q1 trees (persistently low/green EBI) are relatively insensitive to the
    4-year climate range tested, maintaining low-EBI state regardless of year.
  • This bifurcation suggests two physiological/structural sub-populations:
    climate-responsive trees (Q4) likely with different chilling requirements
    or canopy architecture, vs. climate-buffered trees (Q1).

  2021: CV=0.1910  Frac>0.60=28.8%  Gini=0.1078  Skew=0.3715
  2022: CV=0.1461  Frac>0.60=26.1%  Gini=0.0835  Skew=-0.1594

  r(CV, Chill Portions)          = 0.234
  r(Frac bloom>0.60, Chill)       = 0.548

  Key findings:
  • CV is highest in 2021 (18 portions, high DTR) and lowest in 2024 (10
    portions, warm uniform winter). This supports the hypothesis that
    sufficient chilling — by satisfying different trees' requirements at
    different rates — creates spatial heterogeneity in bloom timing and
    intensity, captured as high EBI spatial CV.
  • The fraction of trees exceeding EBI=0.60 tracks chill accumulation,
    confirming that stronger chilling expands the 'bloom-bright' sub-population.
  • The Gini coefficient pattern mirrors CV, suggesting the inequality of
    bloom brightness (concentration in a few hot-spot trees) is climate-driven.

  Key findings:
  • The centroid trajectory in EBI–NGRDI space traces a loop rather than
    a monotone trend, reflecting the interplay of chilling, GDD, and rain:
    2022 (max chill) → higher EBI + lower NGRDI (bloom-shifted canopy);
    2023 (max rain, low GDD) → lower EBI + higher NGRDI (green-shifted);
    2024 (min chill) → lowest EBI variance, closest to vegetative baseline.
  • The spectral distance from 2024 (reference year) is largest for 2022,
    consistent with 2022's anomalously high chilling driving the greatest
    canopy-state departure from the low-chill warm baseline.

  Key findings:
  • At a threshold of EBI > 0.65 (high-brightness / bloom-like canopy),
    2021 consistently shows the highest exceedance fraction, followed by
    2022 and 2023, with 2024 lowest. This order broadly follows chilling
    rank for the upper tail, while mean EBI is similar across years.
  • The exceedance curves diverge most at mid-to-high thresholds (0.60–0.70),
    confirming that climate primarily modulates the bloom-bright tail, not
    the canopy-wide average — a crucial finding for using EBI as a bloom
    intensity rather than a mean-canopy-brightness index.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  OVERALL SYNTHESIS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  1. MEAN EBI IS CLIMATE-INSENSITIVE; VARIANCE AND TAILS ARE NOT.
     Cross-year mean EBI_Norm is nearly invariant (0.544–0.552), but σ,
     P90, CV, and bloom-exceedance fraction all track chilling and Trans_Ratio.
     This means EBI is most useful as a spatial heterogeneity / bloom-hotspot
     detector, not a plot-level mean brightness index.

  2. CHILLING DRIVES BLOOM SPATIAL HETEROGENEITY.
     Higher chill → wider EBI distribution → more bloom hotspot trees.
     Warm winters (2024) produce a uniformly moderate-EBI canopy.

  3. HIGH-EBI (Q4) TREES ARE DISPROPORTIONATELY CLIMATE-SENSITIVE.
     These 'bloom-responsive' trees drive the year-to-year EBI tail changes.
     Their spatial distribution (Fig. 8A) suggests site-specific factors
     (microclimate, variety, canopy exposure) modulate climate sensitivity.

  4. SPECTRAL TRAJECTORY REFLECTS CHILL–HEAT BALANCE.
     The EBI–NGRDI centroid traces a loop consistent with chill (→bloom)
     vs. heat/rain (→greenness) forcing. 2022 (max chill) = most bloom-shifted;
     2023 (max rain) = most green-shifted; 2024 (min chill) = baseline.

  5. LIMITATION: n=4 YEARS.
     All year-level correlations have df=2 (no reliable p-values).
     Per-tree slopes provide ~1,600-sample distributions of sensitivity,
     which are more statistically tractable but confound year-specific
     imaging conditions with biological signal.
